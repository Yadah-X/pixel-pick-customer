Refund Policy

Last updated: 18 November 2026

This Refund Policy explains how Pixel Pick handles payment errors, cancelled competitions and refund requests relating to competition entries.

3.1 When a refund may be considered

  • A payment was processed more than once for the same intended entry or order.
  • A payment was taken but the corresponding entry was not created or confirmed because of a verified technical or processing error.
  • Pixel Pick cancels a competition or is unable to provide the entry or service purchased.
  • A refund is required under applicable South African law, including any cooling-off right under section 44 of the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act.

Each request will be checked against the relevant account, order, payment and entry records.

3.2 Change of mind and unsuccessful participation

A participant is not entitled to a refund merely because they did not win, achieved a lower score than another participant, did not complete a challenge, or changed their mind after participating. This does not affect any refund or cancellation right that applies under South African law, including the cooling-off right described in the Cancellation Policy.

3.3 How to request a refund

Email info@pixelpickcustomer.co.za from the email address associated with your account. Include your name, account email address, competition name, approximate payment date and any available payment or order reference. Do not email passwords, full card numbers or security codes.

3.4 Processing

Pixel Pick will review the request and may ask for reasonable information needed to verify it. Where a refund is approved, it will ordinarily be returned through the original payment method where possible. The time for the funds to appear may depend on the payment provider and the customer's bank. Where a specific statutory refund period applies, that period will be followed.

3.5 Statutory rights

This policy does not remove or restrict any refund, cancellation or other consumer right that applies under the Consumer Protection Act, the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act or other applicable South African law. Statutory rights apply according to the circumstances and any relevant legal exceptions.
